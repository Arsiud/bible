How to use this app

Reading

  • Tap ☰ to choose a book and chapter. Swipe left or right on the text for the next or previous chapter.
  • Hold the Bible tab at the bottom to switch to the Chronological Bible: the same verses, in the order things happened, a passage at a time. Kings and Chronicles take turns reign by reign, the psalms sit beside David's story, the four Gospels are woven into one, and Paul's letters come where he wrote them in Acts. ☰ then lists eras and their passages, and swiping or listening goes on in time order. Hold it again to go back.
  • In the Chronological Bible, each passage you finish (swiping on, tapping the next one, or listening to its end) is marked read. ☰ shows how far you are in each era and in the whole Bible. Hold a passage in an era's list to mark it read or unread; Start over at the bottom clears it. Once you're signed in, it follows you to your other devices.
  • The button at the top right switches translations: GTB (our own Good Tidings Bible), WEB, KJV, ASV, Russian Synodal, and once you're signed in and approved, ESV, NIV, NASB and the Spanish NTV (Nueva Traducción Viviente).
  • The eye sets the look: match your phone, light, dark, gray or a photo of your own, plus text size and spacing.
  • Related passages at the end of each chapter lists other verses on the same things; tap one to go there.
  • The app remembers where you were, on all your devices once you're signed in.

Good Tidings Bible (our translation)

  • GTB, the Good Tidings Bible, is our family's own translation of the King James Version into plain modern English, made to be shared freely: anyone may read, copy and share it, and use it in free apps and websites, without asking (selling it or changing its words needs permission). Its name comes from Isaiah 61:1, the "good tidings" Jesus read at Nazareth as His own mission (Luke 4:18-21). It works offline.
  • A small † after a verse means there's a note on it; α means one of its notes is about the original Hebrew, Aramaic or Greek. Tap it to read them. Tap a reference in a note to go there.
  • Notes on a whole chapter, psalm or book are under About… below the title.
  • Search also looks through the notes, so a word like hesed or agape finds every note on it.
  • To read without the marks, open the eye menu and turn off Translation notes.

Picking verses

  • Hold a verse to pick it. Tap another verse to stretch the pick to it; tap the first or last verse to let it go.
  • To pick just some words, hold a word, then slide your finger to the last word you want without lifting it, like selecting in a Kindle. It can run into the next verses. Tap anywhere to let the words go.
  • The bar at the bottom then offers Note, Bookmark, Copy, Compare (the verses in every translation; tap one to switch to it) and Related (other passages on the same things, with their words). ✕ puts the pick away.

Notes

  • Pick verses and tap Note. Choose who sees it: Just me, or one of your groups.
  • Verses with notes are shaded. Pick one and the bar shows how many notes are on it.
  • A note on picked words shades just those words, and shows them in quotes. In another translation the whole verse is shaded instead.
  • On group notes you can Reply. On your own notes: Edit, Change verses or Delete.
  • Each group on the Connection tab lists all its notes; scroll the box to see older ones. Tap a note to go to its verses, or hold it to reply (or to edit or delete your own).
  • Your Just-me notes are also listed at the bottom of the Bookmarks tab.

Bookmarks

  • The ribbon at the top drops a temporary bookmark where you are; the next tap moves it.
  • To keep one, tap Name on it in the Bookmarks tab, or pick verses and tap Bookmark.
  • Edit a saved bookmark to rename it, add notes, change its verses or delete it.

Search and going to a verse

  • Tap the magnifying glass and type words or a phrase; close matches show too. Words to finish what you're typing show under the box.
  • Misspellings are fixed for you ("rightousness" finds righteousness); tap Search for … instead to keep your spelling. A word finds its other forms too: knows finds "knoweth", says finds "saith".
  • Verses worded your way in another translation on this device show too, marked with that translation.
  • Above the verses: your own notes and bookmarks, topics (try prayer or forgiveness), people and places (Barnabas, Bethel), and Hebrew and Greek words (agape, chesed, or a Strong's number like G26). Tap one for all its verses.
  • The chips narrow the verses to the Old or New Testament, the Gospels, the Letters or the book you're reading; the row under them counts the verses in each book: tap one to see only those.
  • "Quotes" keep words exactly as typed, a minus leaves a word out (love -money), and * stands for any letters (*loved finds "beloved").
  • After opening a result, the bar at the bottom goes to the next or previous one, and the words you searched for stay lit. Tap its middle to see the list again; ✕ puts it away.
  • With the box empty, your recent searches are there to tap again.
  • Or type a reference, like jn 3:16, 1 cor 13 or Ps 23, to go straight there.
  • After jumping somewhere (search, a bookmark, a note), ‹ Back to… at the top takes you back to where you were reading.

Listening

  • Once you're signed in and approved, ▶ Listen plays the ESV read by David Cochran Heath, chapter after chapter. The verse being read is highlighted.
  • While it plays, tap a verse to play from there. The 1× button changes the speed.

Connection

  • Sign in with Google or an email and password. New accounts get 7 days of access while the owner approves them.
  • Create a group or join one with its code or an invite link, and see which chapter everyone is reading.

Without internet

  • Our Good Tidings Bible (with its notes), the WEB, KJV, ASV and Russian Synodal work offline, and the NASB for the family members the owner has allowed.
  • The ESV, NIV, NTV, listening, notes and syncing need a connection; what you do offline is sent once you're back online.

Settings

Not needed once you're signed in and approved on the Connection tab. Or get a free key at api.esv.org for personal, non-commercial use. It's saved only on this device.
